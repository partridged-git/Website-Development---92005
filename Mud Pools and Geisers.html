The mud pool is the result of acid gases and steam that cause the decomposition of minerals (feldspars) to form a clay called kaolin. Kaolin is white when pure, but finely divided black sulphur turns it to grey. The activity varies with the amount of rainfall, but the temperature of the steaming bursts of mud is approximately 90-95 degrees Celsius (194-203 degrees Fahrenheit). This mud pool is also known as the “Frog Pool” because of the similarity between the plopping mud and leaping frogs. Some pools contain mud with medicinal properties that are used to treat skin, rheumatic, and arthritic ailments. Māori traditionally used mud to treat stomach infections by mixing a small amount with water and drinking it.
